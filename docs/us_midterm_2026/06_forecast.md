# 06 — Forecast: 2026 U.S. Midterm Elections

*Prepared **2026-05-29**, incorporating live web-search data (tagged `[LIVE]` in
files 02-04) and prediction-market prices. Built bottom-up (files 02-03),
validated top-down (file 04), gated on evidence quality (file 05).*

## Headline call

> **Most likely outcome (≈50%): divided government** — Democrats **flip the U.S.
> House** (solidly), Republicans **narrowly hold the U.S. Senate**. But the
> Senate is a near-coin-flip, so a **full Democratic sweep is a strong secondary
> scenario (≈30%)**. Confidence: **High** on the House flip; **Lean** only on
> Republicans holding the Senate.

## Seat ranges (not point estimates)

- **House:** a **solid Democratic majority** — central estimate around **D ~233**
  (i.e., roughly **+18 to +23** seats from the start), consistent with the D+6
  generic ballot and Sabato's redistricting-adjusted model. Range ≈ **D 223-240**
  in the base case; a held-but-shrunken GOP majority is the ~22% tail.
- **Senate:** centered near **51-52 R** (Republicans holding by a hair). The
  Democratic-control scenario (≥51 D) requires winning **North Carolina (already
  Lean D) + Maine + Ohio + Alaska while holding Georgia/Michigan/NH/Minnesota** —
  a real ~38% path, no longer a remote tail.

## Why this is the call (one-paragraph logic)

The May-2026 fundamentals are **strongly pro-Democratic and worsening for the
GOP**: Trump approval ≈ **−20 net (lowest of either term)** amid **stagflation
fear** (Q1 GDP +2.0%, PCE +4.5%), and the generic ballot is **D+6-7 (bluest of
the cycle, widest since 2018)** — atop the near-ironclad midterm penalty
(file 04). For the **House**, the GOP's two firewalls (the +10-seat
redistricting cushion and incumbency) are simply **too small** to offset a D+6
environment — Sabato's model shows the cushion trims only ~5 seats, leaving a
~+23 D gain — so the House flip is **high-confidence**. For the **Senate**, the
**structural +4 requirement on red-leaning turf** is a genuine firewall, but it
has been **partly breached** by Tillis's retirement (NC now Lean D with Cooper)
and the Brown (OH) and Peltola (AK) recruitments, turning Republican retention
from "safe" into a **lean** that markets price near a coin flip.

## Races to watch (the forecast's hinges)

- **The Senate runs through six seats:** **NC (Cooper vs. Whatley — likeliest
  flip), Maine (Collins), Ohio special (Brown vs. Husted), Alaska (Peltola vs.
  Sullivan)** for D pickups; **Georgia (Ossoff)** and **Michigan (open)** as D
  must-holds. Democrats need ~4 of the pickups while holding both — that
  conjunction is the whole ballgame.
- **Texas:** Paxton (beat Cornyn) vs. Talarico — Lean R, an upset lane if
  Paxton's baggage bites.
- **House tipping points:** suburban R-held, Harris-won seats in CA, NY, NJ, PA,
  AZ, VA, netted against new R-leaning lines from Texas/Ohio and California's
  counter-map (court rulings here are still moving the odds).
- **36 governorships** (MI most competitive; IA, KS, NH, NV, AZ, PA, WI, GA in
  play) — consequential for the 2030 redistricting cycle.

## What would change this forecast (decision triggers)

Re-run if, by autumn 2026:
1. **Generic ballot collapses toward tied** → House flip becomes a real contest
   (the only thing that saves the GOP House per Sabato's model).
2. **Approval recovers to the mid-40s** → both calls firm up for Republicans.
3. **A president-favoring shock** (rally event) → base rate could break (cf.
   2002).
4. **Courts further reshape the maps** (the May rulings already helped the GOP) →
   adjust House magnitude.
5. **A GA/MI Democratic hold wobbles, or a NC/OH/AK/Maine race breaks clearly one
   way** → flips the Senate call between "R holds" and "D sweep."

## Bottom line (plain language)

The **House will very likely flip to the Democrats** — the pro-D environment is
strong enough that the GOP's redistricting cushion can't hold it back. The
**Senate is a genuine coin-flip leaning Republican**: the structural map favors
the GOP, but Tillis's retirement and the Brown/Peltola recruitments have made
Democratic control a real ~1-in-3-plus possibility. So the single most likely
result is **divided government (Democratic House, Republican Senate)** — but
unlike a normal cycle, **a clean Democratic sweep is very much on the table.**
Hold the House call firmly; hold the Senate call loosely.
