# 02 — The 2026 Senate Map (Class 2 + OH/FL specials)

*Live data via web search on **2026-05-29** marked **[LIVE]**. Sources: NPR
(2026-05-02), Wikipedia/Ballotpedia race pages, Cook/Sabato/Inside Elections
consensus via 270toWin, Emerson, CNN/PBS/NBC primary results. Verify before
relying on specifics.*

## 0. The board — **[LIVE]**
- **35 seats** on the ballot: 33 Class-2 regulars **+ Ohio & Florida specials**.
- **23 of the 35 are Republican-held** — Republicans defend more turf.
- Start **53 R – 47 D**; **Democrats need net +4** to reach 51 and control (VP
  Vance breaks 50-50 for Republicans).
- **11 senators not seeking re-election** (4 D, 7 R). Retiring outright:
  **D** — Durbin (IL), Peters (MI), Smith (MN), Shaheen (NH); **R** — McConnell
  (KY), Tillis (NC), Ernst (IA), Lummis (WY), Daines (MT). Running for governor
  instead: **Blackburn (TN), Tuberville (AL)** (R) and **Bennet (CO)** (D).

## 1. Why the map still favors Republican retention — with caveats

Class 2 was last contested in **2020**, leaving **few exposed Democratic seats**
while the competitive Republican seats sit in **states Trump carried**. To net
**+4**, Democrats must nearly **run the table on red-leaning turf while holding
all their own**. *But* the strongly pro-Democratic 2026 environment (file 04:
approval ≈ −20, generic D+6-7), strong recruits, and a wave of GOP open seats
make the +4 path **more live than any prior point this cycle**, even though
Republicans remain favored to hold.

## 2. Competitive seats (the ones that decide control)

### Republican-held → Democratic targets
- **North Carolina — OPEN (Tillis retiring): Roy Cooper (D) vs. Michael Whatley
  (R).** **[LIVE]** *The most likely seat in the country to flip* — rated
  **Toss-up trending Lean D**. Cooper, a popular former two-term governor who has
  won statewide repeatedly, is a top-tier recruit in a state Trump carried only
  single digits. **The Democrats' #1 pickup.**
- **Maine — Susan Collins (R).** **[LIVE — Toss-up]** Marquee D target in a
  blue-leaning state with an unpopular president. Collins's personal brand is the
  firewall (she survived 2008 and 2020 D waves). D nominee TBD (Graham Platner /
  David Costello primary).
- **Ohio (special) — Jon Husted (R, appt.) vs. Sherrod Brown (D).** **[LIVE —
  newly competitive]** Brown, a former senator with a long statewide-winning
  record, **converts a likely-R hold into a real contest.**
- **Alaska — Dan Sullivan (R) vs. Mary Peltola (D).** **[LIVE — newly
  competitive]** Peltola won statewide in 2022; ranked-choice voting + a proven
  candidate make this a **live, if lean-R, target.**

### Democratic-held → must-hold (Republican targets)
- **Georgia — Jon Ossoff (D).** **[LIVE — Toss-up]** Most vulnerable D incumbent
  (narrow 2021 win, purple state, midterm). The pivotal **must-hold.**
- **Michigan — OPEN (Peters retiring).** **[LIVE — Toss-up]** True swing state.
- **New Hampshire — OPEN (Shaheen retiring).** **Lean D** (R upside if a strong
  moderate runs).
- **Minnesota — OPEN (Smith retiring).** **Lean D.**

### Texas — **[LIVE — primary resolved, still Lean/Likely R]**
- **Ken Paxton (R) beat Sen. John Cornyn** in the **May 26 runoff (63.8%)**,
  Trump-endorsed. Democrat **James Talarico** is the nominee (**Colin Allred
  exited Dec 2025**). General polling: **Cornyn had led Allred 45-38; Paxton
  leads Talarico/Allred by a narrower ~46-41.** Paxton is the weaker general-
  election nominee, so Texas moved **Likely → Lean R (2026-05-26)** — a stretch D
  reach, not a core target, but worth watching as an upset lane.

## 4. Control arithmetic — updated for live data

Start **53 R – 47 D**; Democrats need **net +4**.

- **D pickup lanes now ≈4-5:** North Carolina (**Lean D**), Maine (toss-up),
  Ohio special (toss-up/lean-R), Alaska (lean-R), with Texas a stretch — a
  **deeper field than the ~2 lanes in the January baseline**, thanks to the
  Cooper, Brown, and Peltola recruitments and Tillis's retirement.
- **D must simultaneously hold** Georgia (toss-up), Michigan (toss-up), New
  Hampshire, Minnesota.

The +4 path is now **genuinely available**: roughly *NC + Maine + Ohio + Alaska,
holding everything*. But it still requires Democrats to **win essentially every
toss-up/lean-R seat AND lose none of their own** — a low-but-real-probability
parlay even in a D+6-7 year. **Consensus forecasters still rate the map favorable
to Republicans to hold; prediction markets price R retention at only ~53-55%**
(file 06), reflecting how much the field has tightened.

**Bottom line:** Republicans **favored to hold the Senate, but genuinely
contested** — control is a live ~1-in-3 possibility for Democrats, no longer a
remote tail.
