# 05 — Situational Adequacy Assessment (analytic-confidence gate)

This file is the "状況判断としての適格性" check: before committing to a
forecast, we test whether the evidence is *adequate* to support a judgment, and
at what confidence. It borrows the discipline of formal intelligence analysis
(source reliability, key assumptions, alternative hypotheses, confidence
calibration).

## 1. Source reliability and currency

| Evidence stream | Reliability | Currency | Note |
|-----------------|-------------|----------|------|
| Historical base rates (midterm penalty, approval→seats) | High | Timeless | Strongest leg of the analysis |
| Institutional structure (control thresholds, who's up, the map) | High | Stable | Facts, not estimates |
| Seat-by-seat ratings | Medium-High | **Early 2026** | Move with retirements/primaries |
| Presidential approval / generic ballot levels | Medium | **Early 2026** | Most perishable inputs |
| 2025-26 redistricting outcomes | **Low-Medium** | **Unsettled** | Litigation + late maps |
| Live May-2026 data | **Absent** | — | Network blocked; see README |

**Key limitation:** No live data could be collected (environment network policy
blocks outbound access; confirmed 2026-05-29). Inputs are reliable through
**January 2026** plus timeless base rates. The four-month gap to the forecast
date, and the unsettled redistricting maps, are the dominant uncertainty
sources.

## 2. Key assumptions (and what breaks the forecast if false)

1. **No president-favoring shock** (no 9/11- or impeachment-backlash-scale
   event reversing the national mood). *If false → GOP could defy the base
   rate, as in 2002.*
2. **Approval stays net-negative / sub-~46.** *If it recovers into the high-40s,
   expected House losses shrink toward "hold."*
3. **Redistricting nets Republicans only a modest, bounded number of House
   seats.** *If R redraws are larger and survive courts while CA's counter
   fails, the House flip threshold may not be met.*
4. **No major realignment in midterm turnout composition** beyond the expected
   out-party enthusiasm edge.
5. **The early-2026 seat ratings have not shifted wholesale** by November.

## 3. Alternative hypotheses (explicitly considered, not dismissed)

- **H1 (base case):** D flips House narrowly; R holds Senate. *Best supported.*
- **H2 (GOP overperforms):** Redistricting + approval recovery let R hold the
  House; R holds/extends Senate. *Plausible; the live downside risk to H1.*
- **H3 (D wave):** Approval craters, R retirements cascade → D flips House
  comfortably *and* nets Senate seats (still short of +4 control absent a
  near-sweep of toss-ups). *Lower probability given the Senate map.*
- **H4 (D takes Senate too):** Requires D net +4 — running the table on
  Maine + NC + holding all of GA/MI/NH/MN + an upset (e.g., TX/OH special).
  *Low probability; a tail scenario.*

The existence of credible H2 is why the House call is stated as **"favored,"
not "certain."**

## 4. Confidence calibration

| Judgment | Confidence | Basis |
|----------|-----------|-------|
| Republicans hold the Senate | **High** | Structural map; even a strong D year falls short of +4 |
| Democrats favored to flip the House | **Moderate** | Strong fundamentals vs. redistricting headwind + 4-month data gap |
| Specific seat counts / margins | **Low** | Perishable inputs; ranges only, not point estimates |

## 5. Adequacy verdict

The evidence is **adequate to support directional judgments** (who controls each
chamber) at the confidence levels above. It is **not adequate for precise
point forecasts** (exact seat totals, individual close races), because the most
perishable inputs are four months stale and the redistricting maps are
unsettled. The forecast in file 06 is therefore expressed as **direction +
probability band + seat range**, with explicit triggers that would change the
call. Proceeding on that basis is justified; pretending to more precision would
not be.
