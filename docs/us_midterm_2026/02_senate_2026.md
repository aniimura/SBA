# 02 — The 2026 Senate Map (Class 2 + OH/FL specials)

*Live data via web search on **2026-05-29** marked **[LIVE]**. Other ratings
reflect the analyst's early-2026 reading of the Cook / Sabato / Inside Elections
consensus. Verify live — retirements and primaries move fast.*

## 0. The board — **[LIVE]**
- **35 seats** on the ballot: 33 Class-2 regulars **+ Ohio & Florida specials**.
- **23 of the 35 are Republican-held** — Republicans are defending more turf.
- Starting point **53 R – 47 D**; **Democrats need net +4** to reach 51 and
  control (VP Vance breaks 50-50 for Republicans).
- **11 senators are retiring** (4 Democrats, 7 Republicans) as of May 2026,
  opening multiple seats (incl. Kentucky/McConnell, Michigan/Peters,
  Minnesota/Smith, New Hampshire/Shaheen, Illinois/Durbin).

## 1. Why the map still favors Republican retention — with a caveat

Class 2 was last contested in **2020**, leaving **few exposed Democratic seats**
but placing the competitive Republican seats in **states Trump carried**. To net
**+4**, Democrats must nearly **run the table on red-leaning turf while losing
none of their own**. That is hard — *but* the strongly pro-Democratic 2026
environment (file 04: approval −20, generic D+6-7) plus **two new top-tier
Democratic recruits** (below) make the +4 path **more live than a normal cycle**,
even if Republicans remain favored to hold.

## 2. Competitive seats (the ones that decide control)

### Republican-held, Democratic targets
- **Maine — Susan Collins (R).** Marquee D target; rated **Toss-up**. Collins's
  personal brand is the GOP firewall in a blue-leaning state. **[LIVE: toss-up]**
- **North Carolina — Thom Tillis (R).** Perennial purple-state **Toss-up**.
  (Watch any retirement/primary shifts.)
- **Ohio (special) — Jon Husted (R, appointed) vs. Sherrod Brown (D).**
  **[LIVE — newly competitive]** Brown, a former senator who has repeatedly won
  statewide, is running. His entry **converts a likely-R hold into a genuine
  contest** and adds a 3rd real D pickup target.
- **Alaska — Dan Sullivan (R) vs. Mary Peltola (D).** **[LIVE — newly
  competitive]** Peltola, who won statewide in 2022, is running. Alaska's
  ranked-choice system + a proven D candidate make this a **live target**, a 4th
  realistic D pickup lane.

### Democratic-held, Republican targets (Democrats must HOLD these)
- **Georgia — Jon Ossoff (D).** **[LIVE]** The most vulnerable Democratic
  incumbent (narrow 2021 win, purple state, midterm). **Toss-up** — and a *must-
  hold* for any Democratic majority math.
- **Michigan — OPEN (Peters, D, retiring).** **[LIVE]** True swing state;
  **Toss-up / Lean D**.
- **New Hampshire — OPEN (Shaheen, D, retiring).** **Lean D**, with R upside if a
  strong moderate runs.
- **Minnesota — OPEN (Smith, D, retiring).** **Lean D.**

### Other watch seats
- **Texas — John Cornyn (R)**, in a bruising primary (Ken Paxton). A divisive
  nominee + a strong Democrat could pull it to **Lean R** from safe.
- **Florida special — Ashley Moody (R, appointed).** Red-trending; **Likely R.**

## 4. Control arithmetic — updated for live data

Starting **53 R – 47 D**, Democrats need **net +4**.

- **D pickup lanes now number ~4** (Maine, North Carolina, **Ohio**, **Alaska**)
  — up from ~2 in the analyst's January read, because of the Brown and Peltola
  recruitments.
- **D must simultaneously hold** Georgia (vulnerable), Michigan, New Hampshire,
  Minnesota.

So the +4 path is **arithmetically available** for the first time this cycle —
but it requires Democrats to **win essentially every toss-up AND hold every one
of their own**, a low-probability parlay even in a D+6-7 year. Consensus
forecasters (Sabato, Cook, NPR's framing) still rate the map **favorable to
Republicans to hold**, with Democratic control a **live but uphill** scenario.

**Bottom line:** Republicans remain **favored to hold the Senate**, but 2026 is
**more competitive than the January baseline** — control is no longer a near-
impossibility for Democrats; it is a needle-threading tail with a real, if
minority, probability (quantified in file 06).
