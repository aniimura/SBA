# 02 — The 2026 Senate Map (Class 2 + OH/FL specials)

*As of analyst knowledge (Jan 2026). Ratings reflect the consensus of Cook
Political Report / Sabato's Crystal Ball / Inside Elections style categories as
the analyst understood them in early 2026. Verify live — retirements and primary
results move fast.*

## 1. Why the map favors Republican retention

Class 2 was last contested in **2020**, a year Republicans defended well. The
result is a 2026 map with **few exposed Democratic seats** but where the
**Republican seats in play are in states Trump carried**, so Democratic pickups
require winning hostile turf. To net **+4**, Democrats must run the table on a
handful of reach seats while losing none of their own — a low-probability
parlay.

## 2. Competitive seats (the ones that decide control)

### Republican-held, Democratic targets
- **Maine — Susan Collins (R), running.** The marquee D target. Collins has
  repeatedly outrun her state's presidential lean; a strong D year plus a
  top-tier challenger makes it a genuine **Toss-up to Lean R**. Collins's
  personal brand is the GOP firewall.
- **North Carolina — Thom Tillis (R).** Perennial **Toss-up**. NC is the most
  purple of the GOP-held seats up. (Watch for any Tillis retirement/primary
  developments after Jan 2026, which would reshape this race.)

### Democratic-held, Republican targets
- **Georgia — Jon Ossoff (D), running.** The marquee R target and the single
  most important Democratic-held seat: a purple state, an incumbent who won
  narrowly in a 2021 runoff, defending in a non-presidential year. **Toss-up.**
- **Michigan — OPEN (Gary Peters, D, retiring).** Open seat in a true swing
  state. **Toss-up to Lean D**, depending on nominees.
- **New Hampshire — OPEN (Jeanne Shaheen, D, retiring).** Open but D-leaning.
  **Lean D**, with upside R risk if a strong moderate (e.g., a Sununu-type)
  runs.
- **Minnesota — OPEN (Tina Smith, D, retiring).** Open but D-leaning. **Lean
  D.**

### Other watch seats
- **Texas — John Cornyn (R)**, facing a bruising primary challenge (Ken Paxton).
  Likely **R** in the general, but a divisive nominee + a strong Democrat (a
  Colin Allred-type) could make it **Lean R** rather than safe.
- **Ohio special — Jon Husted (R, appointed).** R-leaning post-2024 Ohio;
  **Likely R**, but specials can surprise and Democrats will contest it.
- **Florida special — Ashley Moody (R, appointed).** Florida has trended red;
  **Likely R.**

## 3. The rest (not expected competitive)

**Safe/Likely R:** Alabama (Tuberville), Alaska (Sullivan), Arkansas (Cotton),
Idaho (Risch), Iowa (Ernst), Kansas (Marshall), Kentucky (**OPEN — McConnell
retiring**, still Safe R), Louisiana (Cassidy), Mississippi (Hyde-Smith),
Montana (Daines), Nebraska (Ricketts; watch for an Osborn-style independent),
Oklahoma (Mullin), South Carolina (Graham), South Dakota (Rounds), Tennessee
(Hagerty), West Virginia (Capito), Wyoming (Lummis).

**Safe/Likely D:** Colorado (Hickenlooper), Delaware (Coons), Illinois (**OPEN —
Durbin retiring**, Safe D), Massachusetts (Markey), New Jersey (Booker), New
Mexico (Luján), Oregon (Merkley), Rhode Island (Reed), Virginia (Warner).

## 4. Control arithmetic

Starting at **53 R – 47 D**, Democrats need **net +4**.

- Realistic D **pickup** opportunities: Maine, North Carolina (≈2 max in a good
  year).
- Realistic R **pickup** opportunities: Georgia, Michigan, New Hampshire,
  Minnesota, Ohio special, Texas (several).

The pool of plausible D gains (≈2) is **smaller** than the pool of plausible D
*losses*. So a strong Democratic national environment most likely yields a
**near-wash or modest D gain (0 to +2)** in the Senate — **short of the +4
needed.** A weak D environment could even **expand** the Republican majority.

**Bottom line:** Republicans are clear favorites to **hold the Senate.** The
live question is the *size* of the majority, not which party controls it.
