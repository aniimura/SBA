# 03 — The 2026 House Outlook

*Live data via web search on **2026-05-29** marked **[LIVE]**.*

## 1. The math is unusually tight

The 119th House began roughly **220 R – 215 D**; **218** is the majority.
**[LIVE]** Democrats **need to flip just 3 seats** to win control — a tiny target.
Midterm history (file 04) says the president's party almost always *loses* House
seats, and the 2026 environment (approval ≈ −20, generic **D+6-7**) is strongly
pro-Democratic. On fundamentals, the House is **the likeliest chamber to flip.**

## 2. The 2025-26 mid-decade redistricting war — **[LIVE]** the GOP firewall

The cycle's defining structural feature is a **mid-decade redistricting arms
race** that moved the starting line before any votes:

- Districts for 2026 use **revised maps from the 2025-26 redistricting** (seats
  still apportioned on the 2020 Census).
- **Republican mid-cycle redraws (Texas, Ohio, and others) net the GOP an
  estimated ~10 House seats** of cushion heading into the election — a far larger
  offset than a typical cycle.
- **Democratic counter-moves** (notably California's ballot-measure redraw)
  partially offset the Republican gains; several maps remain entangled in
  **VRA / partisan-gerrymander litigation**, so the final lines carry residual
  uncertainty.

**Net effect:** the ~10-seat Republican cushion **raises the effective bar**
Democrats must clear and is the single biggest reason a strong national
environment may yield only a **narrow** flip rather than a wave-sized one.

## 3. Where the House is decided

- **R-held seats in Harris-won / even districts** (most flip-prone): suburban
  seats in **California, New York, New Jersey, Pennsylvania, Arizona, Virginia,
  Colorado, Oregon**, plus Iowa/Nebraska/Wisconsin marginals.
- **D-held frontline seats** Republicans defend: a smaller set in PA, NC, WA,
  ME, OH — plus any **newly R-leaning seats created by redistricting**.

## 4. The generic congressional ballot — **[LIVE, 2026-05-29]**

- **Democrats +6 to +7** (Silver D+6.6 — "bluest of the cycle"; FiftyPlusOne D+6;
  US Polling Data D+7).
- A sustained D+6-7 is comfortably in **House-flipping range**. Absent
  redistricting it would imply a **mid-double-digit** D gain; **with** the ~10-
  seat GOP redistricting cushion, the realistic outcome compresses toward a
  **clear-but-not-blowout Democratic majority**.

## 5. House bottom line — **[LIVE]**

- **Direction:** Despite the GOP's redistricting cushion, forecasters
  (Sabato, Cook, Ballotpedia framing) rate **Democrats the *clear favorite*** to
  win the House majority — driven by the midterm penalty, the −20 approval, the
  D+6-7 generic ballot, and a 3-seat flip threshold.
- **Magnitude:** muted versus a textbook wave because of the redistricting
  offset — expect a **decisive-but-modest** flip rather than a landslide.
- This is the **higher-confidence** of the two chamber calls (vs. the Senate),
  and is the central judgment carried into file 06.
