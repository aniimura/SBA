## 1. Starting conditions (the board on which 2026 is played)

The 2024 elections delivered a **Republican governing trifecta**:

- **Presidency:** Donald J. Trump (R), 47th president, inaugurated Jan 20, 2025.
  Vice President JD Vance (R) presides over the Senate and breaks ties.
- **U.S. Senate:** Republican majority, **53 R – 47 D** (the 47 includes
  independents who caucus with Democrats). Republicans flipped West Virginia
  (open, Manchin retired), Ohio (Moreno def. Brown), Montana (Sheehy def.
  Tester), and Pennsylvania (McCormick def. Casey) in 2024.
- **U.S. House:** Republican majority, but **historically narrow** — on the
  order of **220 R – 215 D** at the start of the 119th Congress, a single-digit
  working margin that shrank further with vacancies and special elections.

Two senators left to join the administration, creating 2026 **special
elections**:
- **Ohio (Class 3):** JD Vance's seat → appointee **Jon Husted (R)** must defend
  in a 2026 special.
- **Florida (Class 3):** Marco Rubio's seat (Rubio became Secretary of State) →
  appointee **Ashley Moody (R)** defends in a 2026 special.

## 2. What is on the ballot on November 3, 2026

- **All 435 U.S. House seats.**
- **Senate Class 2** (the ~33 seats last contested in 2020) **plus the OH and FL
  specials.**
- **36 governorships**, including the large prizes of **California, Texas,
  Florida, New York, Georgia, Michigan, Pennsylvania, Wisconsin, Arizona** —
  many open due to term limits (e.g., CA Newsom, TX/FL races) and consequential
  for the 2030 redistricting cycle.
- Thousands of state-legislative seats and ballot measures (several of which,
  in 2025-26, were themselves redistricting measures — see file 03).

## 3. What "winning" requires (the control thresholds)

| Chamber | Current | Threshold to control | Net change needed |
|---------|---------|----------------------|-------------------|
| Senate  | 53 R – 47 D | 50 + VP tiebreak (R) → R need 50; **D need 51** | **D need net +4** |
| House   | ~220 R – 215 D | 218 | **D need ~net +3** |

The asymmetry is the whole story of the cycle:

- **House:** Democrats need only a *small* net gain. With a typical midterm
  penalty against the president's party, this is **the more attainable** target.
- **Senate:** Democrats need a *large* net gain (+4) on a map where most of the
  competitive seats sit in **red-leaning states**. This is **a steep climb**.

## 4. The central question

> *Does the out-party (Democrats) convert the usual midterm backlash into
> divided government by flipping the House, while the Senate map blunts that
> wave and lets Republicans hold (or even extend) the upper chamber?*

The rest of the dossier tests that hypothesis seat-by-seat (files 02-03) and
against fundamentals (file 04), then gates it on evidence quality (file 05)
